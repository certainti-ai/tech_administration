How to use this

This is the opposite of what failed the first time

CRA's finding on both projects was not merely that the work looked like engineering. It was that the T661 section 244 narratives described mechanisms — timestamp skew, message ordering, Kafka runtime uncertainty, schema-agnostic parsers, checkpoint markers, telemetry injection, anomaly clustering, adaptive script logic — that were not demonstrated in the review and were not present in the documents. Writing a more detailed version of the same unevidenced narrative would confirm the Agency's concern rather than answer it, and on a second pass it invites a look at whether the original description was accurate.

So this document does not assert that these experiments happened. It states what each experiment would have to have been, and what record would have to exist, for the claim to be arguable. An engineer who was there fills the verdict row from real artefacts. Anything left unfilled is struck before we write a word to CRA.

How each worksheet is built

Every candidate carries the same six rows. Uncertainty and hypothesis are drafted in the register that would reach CRA — set in serif — but they are conditional on the facts being true. Experiment is what the systematic investigation would have consisted of. Record required is the specific dated artefact, named by system. Where to look gives the repository, tracker or log and a date range. Verdict is empty and stays empty until someone checks.

01

The test we must satisfy

The Advisor works from the five questions in CRA's eligibility policy. Every candidate below is written to answer all five, because failing any one of them ends the argument.

Q1Was there a scientific or technological uncertainty? Not a business constraint, not a lack of access, not a skills gap. Something about the technology itself whose outcome could not be known in advance by a competent professional applying standard practice.
Q2Did the effort involve formulating hypotheses aimed at reducing or eliminating that uncertainty? A specific, stated proposition about cause and effect that could be shown false.
Q3Was the approach consistent with a systematic investigation — formulating and testing hypotheses by experiment or analysis? Iteration alone is not systematic investigation. Trying things until one works is debugging. The sequence must show design, prediction, measurement, and a conclusion that fed the next attempt.
Q4Was it undertaken to achieve a scientific or technological advancement? An advance in the underlying technology base or in knowledge not publicly available and not deducible by a competent professional. Faster validation is a business outcome, not an advancement.
Q5Was a record of the hypotheses tested and the results kept as the work progressed? Contemporaneous. This is the question that decided the first review, and it is the one this document exists to answer.
Two provisions in subsection 248(1) decide these two projects

Paragraph (f) excludes quality control and routine testing. Both projects are, on their face, testing engagements. P1 validates call detail records; P2 is a quality engineering managed service. Read plainly, the testing activity itself is outside the definition — and that, not the absence of sophistication, is the deepest problem in the file.

Paragraph (d) admits support work only when it attaches to eligible work. Computer programming, data collection and testing qualify when they are commensurate with, and directly in support of, experimental development under paragraph (c). Automation scripting does not stand on its own. It needs an eligible core to hang from, and the claim must identify that core explicitly.

02

The reframe that decides both projects

If there is a viable position anywhere in P1 and P2, it rests on one distinction, and everything else in this document is built on it.

The eligible work is not the testing. It is building an instrument that made testing possible.

Executing tests against a system is quality control. Developing a method of measurement where no known method existed — where it was genuinely unknown whether the measurement could be made at all — is experimental development, and the tests then run are its application.

The first submission argued the wrong half. It described faster validation, a golden dataset, and broad automation coverage — all outcomes of testing. The Advisor read exactly that and applied paragraph (f). Every candidate below is therefore framed as instrument development, never as test execution, and any candidate that cannot be framed that way is marked weak on its face.

And there is one genuine technological problem running through both projects

Strip the tooling away and P1's timing buffer and P2's mobile authentication handoff are the same problem: deciding when an asynchronous process has finished, when that process runs inside a system you cannot instrument, observe, or ask. No completion signal, no callback, no queryable state — and a fixed wait is either wrong or wasteful because the distribution of completion times is unknown and non-stationary.

That is a real problem in distributed systems, and it is not solved by standard practice in the general case. Whether Tech Mahindra solved it — or merely padded a sleep until the failures stopped — is a question of fact that only the code history answers. If the record shows the former, it is the strongest single argument available across the whole claim, and it spans both projects. If it shows the latter, both candidates fall and very little remains.

P1

Project 1 — Rogers CBU Wireless Y.IN2100170

Five candidates from the interview record, strongest first. The Advisor has already characterised this work as modernisation, integration and workarounds, so each candidate has to defeat that reading on its own facts, not by assertion.

P1-A

Deriving aggregation behaviour of an inaccessible rating system at runtime

Conditional
Uncertainty

The rating platform was operated by a third party and could not be inspected, instrumented or queried. Its aggregation behaviour — how many raw records it emits for a call of a given duration — was undocumented, and changed during the year without notice. It was not known whether a validation method could be built that stayed correct across such a change, or whether any method must be re-specified by hand each time the vendor altered behaviour that we could not see.

Hypothesis

That the emission interval can be inferred at runtime from the record stream itself — from the relationship between a known generated usage event and the set of records it produces — rather than supplied as a configured constant.

Experiment
  • Generate usage of precisely known duration in the network lab, so the expected record count is determined by the hypothesis rather than observed after the fact.
  • Capture the emitted set and compare count and boundaries against prediction.
  • The falsifying observation on record: two records emitted across a two-minute window where the configured assumption predicted one.
  • Re-specify the derivation, re-predict, re-run across durations spanning the boundary conditions.
Record required
  • The dated defect or ticket raised when validation began failing, with its description of the mismatch — this is the moment the uncertainty was recognised.
  • The log excerpt showing two records where one was expected, with its timestamp.
  • The commit that changed the handling. This one artefact decides the candidate.
Where to look
  • Defect tracker for the RTRM workstream, 2023-04 to 2024-03, searching on interval, aggregation, CDR count, duplicate.
  • Repository history for the validation scripts across the same window.
  • The RTRM daily status reports already in the SharePoint archive — they may date the discovery even where the ticket is gone.
Verdict

This candidate turns entirely on what the commit shows. If the change substituted one constant for another, this is debugging against undocumented behaviour and it is struck — a competent professional finds it and fixes it, which is standard practice. If it introduced runtime derivation so that the validator no longer depended on knowing the interval, that is a method that did not previously exist and the uncertainty above is real.

P1-B

Determining completion of an asynchronous emission with no completion signal

Best available
Uncertainty

Records for a single usage event arrive from upstream network elements over an interval that varies with network conditions, and the emitting systems provide no completion signal, no terminal marker and no queryable state. Validating a complete set therefore requires knowing when the set is complete. It was unknown whether completion could be determined at all from the observable stream, or whether any approach must fall back on a fixed wait — which is either unsafe, because the tail is unbounded, or uneconomic, because it must be sized for the worst case.

Hypothesis

That completion can be inferred from the arrival pattern — that inter-arrival behaviour after the final record is statistically distinguishable from a gap between records — and that a decision rule on that signal is more reliable than any fixed delay.

Experiment
  • Instrument arrival timestamps per usage event and characterise the delay distribution, rather than assuming one.
  • Test fixed waits across a range, measuring the false-completion rate at each — establishing that the failure is a property of the approach, not of the parameter.
  • Implement and test the inference rule against the same corpus; compare error rates.
  • Re-test under degraded network conditions, where the distribution shifts, to establish whether the rule holds.
Record required
  • Measured arrival-delay data. Any file, chart or log that shows the distribution was characterised rather than guessed. This is the single most valuable artefact in the entire claim.
  • Evidence of more than one wait value tried with recorded outcomes.
  • Commit history showing how the delay was arrived at — a value that appears once, fully formed, argues against experimentation as strongly as a series of adjustments argues for it.
Where to look
  • Repository history filtered to the wait, sleep, buffer or delay constant. The commit sequence is the experimental record, whether or not anyone intended it as one.
  • Test result archives for runs that failed on incomplete record sets.
  • Any spreadsheet or notebook where someone worked out the timing — informal analysis still counts under Q5 if it is dated.
Verdict

Framed as instrument development this survives paragraph (f) cleanly: the eligible work is establishing a method for determining completion, and the CDR validation is its application. It is also the candidate that pairs with P2-A to show the same technological problem attacked in two independent environments — which is materially harder for the Advisor to dismiss than either alone.

P1-C

Reconstructing event records from a log stream during partial integration

Weak on its face
Uncertainty

With the notification path not integrated end to end, the event records it would have produced did not exist. It was unknown whether records of sufficient fidelity to validate downstream behaviour could be reconstructed from log output alone, given that logs are lossy with respect to ordering and carry no delivery semantics.

Hypothesis

That a reconstruction from log entries preserves the properties downstream validation depends on, and that any loss is bounded and characterisable.

Experiment
  • Reconstruct against a case where the true record set is independently known, and measure divergence.
  • Establish which properties survive reconstruction and which do not.
Record required
  • Any fidelity comparison between reconstructed and true records. Without it there is no experiment, only a workaround.
  • A design note stating what the reconstruction was known not to preserve.
Where to look
  • Repository history for the log-parsing scripts; design pages in the project space.
Verdict

Do not lead with this. The team itself described these scripts to the Advisor as temporary workarounds pending integration, and he recorded that characterisation. Reopening it without a fidelity analysis in hand invites him to repeat his own finding back to us. Include only if the comparison exists.

P2-A

Handing control between two automation runtimes with independent session state

Only real candidate
Uncertainty

An authentication flow spans a web application and a mobile application driven by two separate automation runtimes, each holding its own session context, with no shared process, no shared memory and no inter-process contract between them. The web driver must suspend until an action completes inside the mobile runtime, and neither framework exposes a mechanism for one to observe completion in the other. It was unknown whether deterministic handoff between them was achievable, or whether cross-runtime coordination is only ever approximated by waiting.

Hypothesis

That a coordination channel external to both runtimes can carry a completion assertion reliably enough to sequence them deterministically, and that correctness under this scheme does not depend on the relative speed of the two runtimes.

Experiment
  • Establish the failure characteristics of fixed waits — the team already told the Advisor these were unsuitable, which is a finding and should be presented as one, with the measurements that led to it.
  • Design a channel — token file, shared state record, signalling service — and specify the completion assertion it carries.
  • Test under deliberately skewed timing, forcing each runtime to be the slower one, to establish whether correctness is independent of relative speed.
  • Measure the residual failure rate against the fixed-wait baseline.
Record required
  • The synchronisation code and its full history in Bitbucket. The commit sequence is the experimental record; a mechanism that appears in one commit and never changes suggests a known pattern applied, while a sequence of reworks suggests investigation.
  • Defect or story records in Jira or ALM describing intermittent authentication failures before the mechanism existed, and their closure after.
  • Any pass-rate comparison across the change.
  • Design discussion in the Confluence space — the QAT space already holds framework material, so a design page may well exist.
Where to look
  • confluence.agile.bns, QAT space — framework and tool-stack pages are already known to be there.
  • Bitbucket, the Quantum framework repository, 2023-04 to 2024-03, filtered to the mobile authentication path.
  • ALM / Xray execution history for the affected suites either side of the change.
Verdict

If the mechanism is a polling loop with backoff, it is a documented pattern and the candidate is struck. If it is a coordination protocol the team designed because no pattern fitted, it holds — and it pairs with P1-B as the same technological problem met in an unrelated environment. That pairing is the strongest single argument in the file, because it shows a problem being attacked rather than a story being told.

P2-B

A common execution abstraction over runtimes with incompatible state models

Conditional
Uncertainty

End-to-end validation had to traverse mobile, web, service and mainframe layers, driven by runtimes with incompatible execution and state models — and, as the team noted, none of them providing a store through which state could be handed on. It was unknown whether a single abstraction could carry state across all four without the semantics of any one layer being lost in translation.

Hypothesis

That a common state representation exists which each runtime can produce and consume without loss, and that a shared execution contract can be defined over runtimes whose native models do not correspond.

Experiment
  • Define the state representation and the contract; implement adapters per runtime.
  • Establish, per layer, what the abstraction cannot express — the negative result matters as much as the positive one.
  • Test a traversal that crosses all four layers in a single scenario and verify state integrity end to end.
Record required
  • A dated design document for the framework showing the abstraction was designed rather than accreted.
  • Evidence of a rejected approach — an architecture decision record, a superseded design, a discussion thread. Rejection evidence is what distinguishes design from assembly.
  • The repository history of the framework's core, as distinct from the test scripts written on top of it.
Where to look
  • Confluence QAT space — the tool-stack and framework pages, and anything on knowledge management around them.
  • Bitbucket, framework core, initial commits and subsequent restructures.
Verdict

The Advisor read this as orchestration of existing tools, and integration of commercially available tools is not advancement. The distinction that could carry it is state semantics across incompatible models, not tool coverage. Note the risk: the tool-stack material already in Confluence is dated late 2022 and early 2023, so some of it predates the claim year and cannot support FY2024 work.

Search the history, not the head

What matters is not what the code looks like now but how it got there. A repository whose current state shows a clean, well-chosen mechanism proves nothing; the sequence of commits that arrived at it is the experimental record, and it exists whether or not anyone was keeping one deliberately. Pull the full log for the relevant files across the claim year before concluding anything is missing.

The same applies to tickets closed as duplicates or won't-fix, and to superseded pages in Confluence. Abandoned approaches are the most probative artefacts available, because they are the ones that show an outcome was uncertain.

Two constraints on anything found

It has to fall inside the claim year — 1 April 2023 to 31 March 2024. Some of the Scotia framework material already in hand is dated late 2022, and the evidence deck filed in May quotes a delivery timeline running into April 2024. Neither supports FY2024 work.

It has to be contemporaneous. Anything written now to describe what happened then is a reconstruction, and must be labelled as one. A reconstruction resting on dated artefacts is legitimate and is what a representation is made of. A reconstruction resting on recollection is what the Agency already declined to accept.

05

Three honest outcomes

Decide which one applies per project once the hunt is done, and say so plainly. All three are defensible positions; only pretending decides badly.

Substantiate — artefacts support the uncertainty as stated

Write the representation from the artefacts outward. Each claimed uncertainty cites a dated record; each mechanism named in section 244 is either evidenced or expressly withdrawn. Expect a narrowed claim — the eligible portion is the instrument development, not the full project effort, and saying so before the Advisor does is worth more than the difference.

Narrow — the work happened but the record is thin

Claim only the subset that artefacts reach, at a proportion the evidence carries, and state the method openly as a present-day reconstruction from contemporaneous records. A small well-evidenced claim survives review. A large one resting on the same evidence does not, and costs credibility on the years that follow.

Concede — the artefacts are not there

Then concede these two projects cleanly and in writing, and spend the effort on the five projects denied for documentation rather than on merit. A concession made before being forced is worth real goodwill with the same office that will review FY2025. On the evidence in hand today this remains the most likely outcome for Project 2, and the hunt is what would change that.
