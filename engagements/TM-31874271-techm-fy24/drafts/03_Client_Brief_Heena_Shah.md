## 2. Two different failures, not one

The seven projects failed for two entirely different reasons, and conflating them would cost us.

### Projects 1 and 2 — reviewed, and found not eligible

Rogers CBU Wireless ($740,825) and CBT Scotia QA Managed Service ($559,305) were reviewed technically on May 5 and 7, 2026. The Research and Technology Advisor concluded that the work was system modernisation, integration and configuration of existing tools, and that no technological uncertainty was encountered.

The more serious finding is the second one. The CRA states, in writing and for both projects, that the mechanisms described in section 244 of the filed project descriptions — timestamp skew, message ordering, Kafka runtime uncertainty, schema-agnostic parsers, checkpoint markers, telemetry injection, anomaly clustering, adaptive script logic, hypothesis-driven experimentation — were **not demonstrated in the review meetings and not present in the documents submitted**, which were architecture slides, status decks, dashboards and screenshots.

That is a credibility finding, not just an eligibility finding. It is the reason we will not re-argue those narratives. Restating them without dated evidence would weaken every other position we take, on this year and on future years.

The CRA's own working paper records the reason: the letter of May 7, 2026 asked for a breakdown of monthly hours per employee, split between SR&ED and non-SR&ED work, for each project, plus support for the contractor costs. A response was filed on June 4, 2026 covering Projects 1 and 2. **Nothing was submitted for Projects 3 to 7.** Separately, all $304,752 of arm's-length contract costs across all seven projects were disallowed for want of agreements, invoices, hour allocations and evidence of the taxable-supplier status of the ultimate performers.

These five projects have not been found ineligible. They have been found undocumented. That distinction is the whole of our opportunity.

## 3. What can realistically be recovered

If every dollar of Projects 3 to 7 were substantiated **and** then survived the eligibility review that has not yet happened, the arithmetic yields approximately **$250,795** in credits — roughly $202,000 federal and $48,800 Ontario. That is the ceiling, not a forecast.

We want to be plain about the conditions attached to it:

- The records must exist and be capable of reconciling to the amounts on the filed T661. Reconstructed estimates will not carry.
- Substantiation only opens the door. Projects 3 to 6 are Rogers QE and R4B workstreams that resemble the fact pattern the Advisor has already ruled against on Project 1. Some or all may not survive an eligibility review.
- Project 7 is the strongest of the group: it is salary-only, so there is no contractor documentation problem, and it sits outside the Rogers pattern already judged.
- The contractor costs are the weakest component throughout. Where the ultimate performer of the work is not a taxable supplier — which offshore performers generally are not — the expenditure fails the qualified-expenditure test regardless of the paperwork.

**There is a credible path to recovering nothing. There is no credible path to recovering everything.** We would rather tell you that now than in October.

## 4. The one question that decides this — answer needed within 48 hours

> **Do per-employee, per-project time records for Projects 3 to 7 exist in any Tech Mahindra system for the year ended March 31, 2024?**

Specifically, for the employees whose salaries make up the $814,185 above, can you produce, for each month of FY2024:

- the employee's name and role;
- the project they charged to, identified by the project codes above;
- hours worked, split between SR&ED and non-SR&ED activity;
- the system the data comes from, so the extract can be sourced and stood behind.

Please check with the owners of your time and resource systems — Jira, Clarity, SAP timesheets, workforce or resource-management tooling, and delivery-level project trackers — and tell us plainly whether the data exists, whether it is complete, and how long an extract would take.

**If the answer is yes**, we launch the evidence sprint immediately and we have a case worth making.

**If the answer is genuinely no**, our advice will be to stop spending on the FY2024 defence and redirect that effort into FY2025 and later years. We would rather give you that advice than bill for a submission that cannot succeed.
